  • Cube objects draw in far fewer pieces. A big cube object was thousands of small meshes, each drawn on its own, in every view and every shadow. 0.24.3 preview — 2026-11-13: A warning for teleports SurfsUp may miss

  • A teleport that is thin (under 6 m across in some direction) and whose box touches solid geometry is flagged: it turns red, its tag in every view reads "⚠ ... may be missed at speed", its info panel says why, and the SurfsUp export window lists it. In SurfsUp a fast player (2000+ u/s) can hit that wall before the teleport fires and arrive standing still; MALLET's play mode catches it since 0.24.2. Make it 6 m deep or more, or move it off the wall.

0.24.2 preview — 2026-11-13: Teleports at speed (fix)

  • Fixed: going through a thin teleport whose box runs into a wall at high speed (about 2000+ u/s, depending on the gap) sent you on standing still. Now this tick's move counts: your body (not just its middle) touching the teleport on the way sends you on at full speed. A teleport behind a wall still can't be reached through it. Boosters get the same check, so a thin one is felt at any speed.
  • New test: tp_speed_test (a 3 m teleport into a wall, 1000-6000 u/s; the old code stopped you from 2600 u/s).

0.18.17 preview — 2026-11-11: No more being thrown down the ramp

  • Play mode: flicking away from a ramp mid-surf (a j-turn) could throw you down the ramp and end the run. The movement code (SUCC) tilted your air strafe to follow the ramp's face, so a flick away from the ramp pointed down the slope; if you were still moving up at that moment, one tick could take away all of your climb (9 m/s in one tick at 10 m/s up a 60-degree ramp). Strafing into a ramp while falling did the opposite and canceled the fall. Air strafing is now horizontal, as in Source and SurfsUp; a ramp only redirects your speed along its face. A flick now costs nothing but gravity, and strafing into a ramp gives exactly what Source gives.
  • Straight surfing is unchanged (2,137 u/s after a 76 m drop; SurfsUp measured about 2,200), and the physics tick rate makes no difference to it.
  • New test: surf_flick_test.

0.18.15 preview — 2026-11-11: Maps of big blocks are fast again

  • Maps built from big cubes (made before 0.18.12) ran slowly when you looked toward the build, and in play mode the player couldn't move. 0.18.12 cut every cube object into 4 m pieces so big edits redraw quickly, but a cube goes in the piece it starts in, so a map of spread-out big blocks became one piece per block: a 76,000-cube map made 14,700 pieces (about 7 fps), more physics bodies than the physics engine allows, and the player was never created properly. Pieces now come in two sizes by cube size: cubes of 0.5 m or less (generated shapes, detail) in 4 m pieces, bigger cubes in 16 m pieces. That map is now 850 pieces at about 100 fps, and big edits stay smooth.
  • The physics engine now allows 65,536 bodies (it was 10,240), so a very big map can't stop the player again.
  • Lava and death material inside cube objects kills in play mode again (it was dropped with an error).
  • New test: chunk_budget_test.
