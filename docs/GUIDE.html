The MALLET guide

MALLET (Mapping And Level Layout Editor Tool) makes surf maps for SurfsUp and Counter-Strike: Source. This guide takes you from an empty map to one your friends can play, then covers everything else MALLET can do. Every key is listed at the end.

1. Start

Unzip the whole MALLET folder somewhere and keep MALLET.exe, MALLET.pck and the materials folder together. Double-click MALLET.exe. Windows may say "Windows protected your PC" because the program isn't signed: click More info, then Run anyway.

The welcome screen offers ready-made maps. Each one is a complete map you can play straight away (P):

Selecting and moving. Click an object to select it (Ctrl+click adds or removes one; drag on empty space in a 2D view to box-select); drag it to move it (Shift while dragging: up and down). Arrow keys and Page Up / Page Down nudge the selection by the grid step. Snap to grid (top bar) makes points land on the grid. Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo and redo; Ctrl+S saves (.mallet files).

Locking. Click the padlock next to anything in the Outliner (or right-click > Lock) to lock it: it can't be moved, edited or deleted, and clicks in the views go straight through it to what's behind. Lock a folder and everything in it is locked. A locked object can still be selected in the Outliner (the side panel says it's locked and has an Unlock button), copied, hidden, renamed and filed. In build mode a locked cube object can't be changed either.

2. A first map in ten minutes

  1. Pick Blank on the welcome screen. You get a start platform (green) and a finish (blue).
  2. Press N for a ramp. It appears in front of the camera. Drag its round points in the Top view to aim it, and in the Front or Side view to drop it downhill.
  3. Watch the jump arc (the dotted line off the end of the ramp): it is where a player at your map's speed would fly. Move the next ramp, or the finish, under it.
  4. Press N again for the next ramp, or select a ramp and use Edit > Extend ramp to carry it on.
  5. Press P to play from the start. Surf it. Esc brings you back.
  6. Ctrl+S to save, then Export > To SurfsUp… and type a name. In SurfsUp, open the console (~) and type map <name>.

3. Ramps

Select a ramp to see its settings in the panel.

  • Shape: a V ramp (surf either side) or a half ramp (one side only); angle and face length for how steep and how wide; Solid gives it a flat bottom, Hollow leaves it open underneath.
  • Path: Straight, rounded at the points (new ramps: straight runs with a round corner at each point) or Smooth curve through the points (older maps).
  • Points: drag them. Click a point and press L to lock it so its section keeps its own settings. Edit > Add bend to ramp adds a point in the middle.
  • Pitch, yaw and roll turn the whole ramp; roll banks it.
  • Look: a color, a built-in look (ceramic, grid, neon...), an image of yours, a material from the library, or your own shader. The texture settings turn, shift, scroll it or make it see-through.

Generate a ramp shape (R) builds whole runs (curves, spirals, zig-zags) from a few numbers.

The ramp creator (Add menu, or the button in a ramp's panel) shapes a ramp's cross-section: drag points on its faces, give it a flat top, and shape each end (tapered, rounded, flared, cut diagonally, a lip that launches riders up, or closed). Orange marks parts too flat to surf. Save designs as ramp prefabs (.malletramp files) to reuse and share; eleven come with MALLET.

Built your own start or finish out of ramps or cubes? Start / Finish / Stage zone (on your own platform) adds just the zone box. Zones can be stretched by the small squares on their sides.

5. Playing your map

  • P plays from the start. In build mode, Ctrl+P plays from where you are.
  • R restarts, Backspace goes back to your last stage, Esc returns to the editor.
  • Your best run is kept with the map as a ghost; View > Best run path draws it.

6. Build mode: building with cubes

Press E (or Build in the top bar). You fly in first person and build with cubes, the way Cube 2: Sauerbraten's editor works. F1 shows every key on screen. Each build is a cube object: back in the editor you can move, turn and copy it like anything else (N in build mode starts a new one).

The basics:

  • Aim at a face and roll the mouse wheel: cubes come out of it (or go back in).
  • G + wheel changes the grid (the cube size). F + wheel pushes a face in 1/8 at a time; Q + wheel one corner. Middle drag selects corners to shape.
  • Left drag selects faces; right click extends the selection; right drag moves the selection box (with Shift: leaves a copy). C / V copy and paste, Delete deletes, Z / I undo and redo.
  • Y + wheel changes texture, Y picks up the one you aim at, F2 opens the texture menu, F3 the texture settings (scale, turn, shift, fit to the selection, presets).
  • H (or hold Alt) is the heightmap: shape hills with the wheel; B + wheel sets the brush. Shift+H is an experimental heightmap that adds random shapes.
  • T opens the menu for materials (water, lava, glass, clip, death...), fills, replace, and placing kill zones.
  • Tab places lights, platforms and zones; 1-4 + wheel changes their settings. F4 is the light panel.
  • F6 is the geometry menu: spheres, cylinders, cones, ramps, stairs, quarter pipes, halfpipes and more, sized and turned with sliders, saved as presets.

7. How it looks

Materials. A ramp's or block's Look can be a material: hundreds of real textures (concrete, metal, wood, glowing signs...) in the material picker, with a search box. In build mode the texture menu is the same library. If Counter-Strike: Source is installed, its textures appear too. + Your own image… adds any PNG / JPG / WebP.

The material maker makes your own materials or changes existing ones. In the material picker click Make / edit a material…, or right-click any material to start from it.

  • Start from a material (or a plain color), then stack layers: color changes (hue, saturation, brightness, contrast), fills, stripes and lines, rectangles, ellipses, text, images and painting.
  • Every layer has a color, opacity and blend (mix, add, multiply, subtract). Move, hide, copy or delete layers any time; nothing is final until you save, and even then you can open it again. Adjust colors also has gamma (below 1 lighter, above 1 darker; type any number).
  • Finish (the ball next to it shows it in 3D): Brightness x (how much light it gives back; type 10 or 100 for more), Light of its own (it shines even in the dark; no upper limit), Glossiness (1 = mirror-smooth) and Reflectiveness (1 = a perfect mirror, like polished chrome). Turn on Reflections of the map in the Map panel and shiny materials reflect the map itself, not only the sky.
  • Paint: add a Paint layer, then drag on the picture. Shift+drag paints a straight line, Ctrl+Z undoes a stroke.
  • A line along a ramp's ridge: click + Ridge line. It turns on Fit across a ramp: on ramps the picture spans the riding surface once from edge to edge, so the middle is the ridge (the Ramp guide shows it). Lines at the very left and right run along the ramp's edges.
  • Saved materials appear under Made by you. Save changes it everywhere it's used; Save as new keeps both.
  • Your materials (and images you added) travel inside the maps that use them, so a map opens the same on a friend's PC.

Moving textures: a ramp's texture settings (and F3 in build mode) can scroll across and along, spin (degrees a second) and swirl (twist the middle into a spiral). Each repeat turns around its own middle: use Fit to selection in F3, or a material that fits across a ramp, to turn a whole face as one. Every number box takes any value you type, and Stop moving in F3 sets scroll, spin and swirl back to 0. (A face keeps its texture settings when you change its texture, so a spinning face goes on spinning with the new one.)

The map's look (the panel with nothing selected): the sky (MALLET's own, or your sky shader), sun, fog, Reflections of the map, and the usual look of build mode's water, lava and glass. Any fill can have its own look instead: in build mode's T menu, Fill tab, pick the look under "Their look" before you fill, so every pool can be different.

Lights (U, or Tab in build mode): point lights, spot lights and light strips (neon, tubes), with presets (torch, campfire, alarm, party...), effects (flicker, pulse, strobe) and color cycles. Particles: fire, smoke, sparks, snow, rain, mist and more. Text (X) puts signs on surfaces. Props (I) are glTF models. Water (H) is a water block with presets.

Your own shaders: View > Your shaders folder… Put Godot .gdshader files there: sky shaders show up under the map's Sky, surface shaders in a Look ("Your own shader"). See docs/SHADERS.md.

8. Working with others

Test builds. Export > Test build for testers… makes a zip you can send to anyone: a test program with no editor that drops them on your map and records every attempt (time, splits, falls, route) in a "tester runs" folder. Get that folder back and use Export > Import tester runs…; View > Testers' paths draws their routes.

Live playtesting. Export > Playtest session… (leave the code empty) opens a session. Test builds made while it is open report live: the Playtest panel shows who is on, where they are, their attempts and times, and the 3D view shows each tester as a dot with a trail.

Co-editing. Start a session the same way, then Copy invite on the Playtest panel and send it. The other person opens Export > Playtest session…, ticks Join as a co-editor and clicks Paste invite. From then on both copies of the map change together: every edit, build mode cubes, materials, the map's sky. You see each other's camera, mouse and selection. Undo only takes back your own work. The map's owner can remove someone.

9. Exporting

  • Export > To SurfsUp… puts the map into SurfsUp's Maps folder (SurfsUp must be installed). Everything goes in: ramps, cubes, materials (yours too), lights, zones, teleports.
  • Export > To Counter-Strike: Source… writes a .vmf and can compile it into a playable .bsp (CS:S must be installed). Quick compile is for testing, Final compile for the real thing. The map's own sky can become the skybox. Note: CS:S teleports stop the player; MALLET warns you where that matters.

Both exports check the map first and tell you about anything missing (no finish, a teleport with nowhere to go...).

11. Good to know

  • Mouse sensitivity uses Source's numbers, so your CS:S sensitivity feels the same (View > Mouse sensitivity).
  • With nothing selected, the panel tells you how heavy the map is (lights, particles, cubes).
  • Lights with shadows draw them only while you're within 60 m of the light (it still lights everything farther away), and the flat views show no light shadows: that keeps big maps with many lights smooth.
  • Maps are plain .mallet files: back them up like any document. Older SurfForge .sfmap maps still open.
  • Something broke or felt wrong? Tell whoever sent you MALLET: what you did, what you expected, what happened.
